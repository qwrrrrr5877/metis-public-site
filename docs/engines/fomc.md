# FOMC Engine

<p class="metis-section-label">CentralBank</p>

The FOMC engine studies central-bank meeting structure: rate decisions, voter
thresholds, dissent direction, statement wording, dot-plot distribution, and
minutes language.

## What It Tries To Judge

- When a consensus rate decision hides a second-order object worth modeling.
- Whether a policy preference is likely to become a formal dissent.
- Whether statement language creates a separate vote-topology problem.
- How minutes quantifiers should inform, but not mechanically determine, vote
  interpretation.

## What It Does Not Try To Do

- Compete on obvious consensus rate decisions.
- Treat every public preview as registration-quality evidence.
- Collapse all dissent into a single count.
- Promote a one-meeting communication style into a durable regime thesis.

## Current Public Lesson

The recent April-to-June sequence sharpened a useful distinction: a correct
narrow dissent-count view can still miss the broader structure of the meeting.
April showed a layered split around statement-bias language. June showed that
this topology can disappear cleanly rather than becoming the new default.

## Next Human Review Focus

The next important review should read the June minutes through three questions:

- Was unanimity genuine at the deliberation level, or only at final-vote level?
- Did participants still prefer different statement-bias language?
- Does the shorter statement style represent a durable communication shift or a
  one-meeting reset?
